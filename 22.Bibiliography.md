Appendix: Bibliography and Further Reading
No physicist works in a vacuum. The ideas in this book stand on the shoulders of giants—mathematicians, neuroscientists, and computer scientists who laid the theoretical rails we ride on today.

If you want to dive deeper into the raw equations and primary sources, here is your reading list.

Foundational Papers (The Primary Sources)
These are the "Einsteins" of our field. If you really want to understand the physics, read the original manuscripts.

Attention Is All You Need (2017)

Vaswani, Shazeer, et al.

The Physics: The paper that introduced the Transformer and killed Recurrence. It defined the "Gravitational Pull" (Attention) between words.

Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition (2015)

He, Zhang, Ren, & Sun

The Physics: The invention of ResNets. This solved the "Vanishing Gradient" problem, allowing signals to travel through deep matter without decaying.

Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (2020)

Ho, Jain, & Abbeel

The Physics: The thermodynamics of image generation. It proved that reversing entropy (turning noise into order) is a viable generative strategy.

Playing Atari with Deep Reinforcement Learning (2013)

Mnih et al. (DeepMind)

The Physics: The moment Q-Learning met Neural Networks. The birth of modern Deep RL.

Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models (2022)

Wei et al.

The Physics: The discovery that intermediate steps stabilize the logical trajectory of an LLM.

Textbooks (The Manuals)
Deep Learning (The "Goodfellow Book")

Ian Goodfellow, Yoshua Bengio, and Aaron Courville

Why read it: This is the Bible of the field. It covers the rigorous math of optimization, probability, and regularization.

Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction

Richard S. Sutton and Andrew G. Barto

Why read it: The definitive guide to Agents, Rewards, and the Bellman Equation.

Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning

Christopher Bishop

Why read it: A heavy focus on the Bayesian (Probabilistic) view of AI.

The Feynman Lectures on Physics

Richard Feynman

Why read it: Not about AI, but about thinking. To understand gradients, energy landscapes, and entropy truly, you need to understand the physical world first.

Technical Documentation (The Lab Equipment)
Don't just read books; read the manuals of the tools you use.

PyTorch Documentation (pytorch.org/docs)

The best place to understand autograd and dynamic computation graphs.

Hugging Face Course (huggingface.co/course)

A practical, hands-on guide to Transformers and Tokenizers.

LangChain Documentation (python.langchain.com)

The blueprint for building Agents and connecting tools.

Philosophy and Future (The Theory)
Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies

Nick Bostrom

The Physics: A look at the "Alignment Problem" and what happens when the optimization power of the machine exceeds the control of the operator.

The Master Algorithm

Pedro Domingos

The Physics: A high-level overview of the five tribes of Machine Learning (Symbolists, Connectionists, Evolutionaries, Bayesians, and Analogizers).
